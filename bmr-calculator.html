What is BMR?

Your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) is roughly how many calories your body burns just staying alive — breathing, circulating blood, repairing cells — if you did nothing but rest all day. This calculator uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, which most dietitians consider more accurate than older formulas. Add your activity level on top of BMR to get your full daily maintenance calories (TDEE).

This calculator uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, published in 1990 and generally considered more accurate for modern populations than the older Harris-Benedict equation from 1919, which tends to overestimate BMR for a lot of people. Either way, BMR by itself isn't a calorie target — it's the calorie floor your body needs just to keep functioning at rest, before you add anything for movement, digestion, or exercise on top.

BMR — common questions

Should I eat exactly my BMR number of calories?

No — eating at BMR alone ignores all the calories you burn through daily movement and exercise. Use the Calorie or TDEE calculator instead to get a realistic daily target.

Does BMR change as you get older?

Yes, BMR tends to decline gradually with age, mostly because muscle mass naturally decreases over time — which is part of why calorie needs shift across decades even at a stable weight.
