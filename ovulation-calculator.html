How ovulation is estimated

This assumes a standard 28-day cycle and places ovulation 14 days after the first day of your last period. Real cycles vary in length, so if you know your own average cycle length, use it to adjust the estimate.

The fertile window extends beyond the single estimated ovulation day — typically the 5 days before ovulation plus the day itself, since sperm can survive for several days waiting for an egg to be released. Basal body temperature and cervical mucus changes are commonly used alongside date-based estimates like this one to narrow down the fertile window more precisely, especially for cycles that don't follow a strict 28-day pattern.

Ovulation — common questions

How many days am I actually fertile, not just the one estimated day?

Around 6 days total — the 5 days leading up to ovulation plus the day of ovulation itself, since sperm can survive for several days awaiting the egg.

What if my cycle isn't exactly 28 days?

The 14-day-after-last-period estimate assumes a 28-day cycle. If yours runs longer or shorter, ovulation shifts correspondingly — tracking your actual average cycle length gives a more accurate estimate than the default assumption.
