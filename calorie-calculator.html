What are BMR and TDEE?

Your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) is roughly how many calories your body burns just staying alive — breathing, circulating blood, repairing cells — if you did nothing but rest all day. Your Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) adds your activity level on top of that, giving a realistic estimate of how many calories you actually burn in a normal day. This calculator uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, which most dietitians consider more accurate than older formulas.

The five numbers this calculator shows are starting points, not commands. ’Mild weight loss’ (a ~250 kcal daily deficit) is built for a slow, easier-to-sustain pace — roughly a quarter to half a kilogram a week — while the ’weight loss’ number (a ~500 kcal deficit) targets closer to half a kilogram a week. Both assume the deficit is consistent day to day, which in practice means tracking for a couple of weeks and adjusting based on what the scale actually does, not just trusting the formula blindly.

Calories (BMR + TDEE) — common questions

How much weight will I actually lose eating at my 'weight loss' number?

Roughly 0.4–0.5 kg a week is the common estimate for a 500-kcal daily deficit, since about 7,700 kcal equals 1 kg of body fat — but water retention, hormones, and activity changes mean any single week can look different from the average.

Why do I feel hungrier than expected at my calculated target?

Calorie needs from a formula don't account for sleep quality, stress, protein intake, or how filling your specific food choices are — all of which affect hunger independently of the total number.
